# Jet Engine Playground — factual sources

Research date: 20 November 2026. Linked sources are references, not redistributed assets. Wording and diagrams are original. Links may change; the cited issue and section identify the reviewed material.

## Rolls-Royce Trent XWB-84 / Airbus A350-900

- [Rolls-Royce, Trent XWB](https://www.rolls-royce.com/products-and-services/civil-aerospace/widebody/trent-xwb.aspx): manufacturer and XWB-84 application on the A350-900. The XWB-97 / A350-1000 is outside this simulator's representative variant.
- [UK CAA, UK.TC.E.00058 issue 03, 30 October 2026](https://www.caa.co.uk/Documents/Download/3939/984aa08e-5352-4da9-824b-309ec7cec37d/4022), section III.2, page 7: three coaxial shafts; LP turbine drives fan, IP turbine drives IP compressor, HP turbine drives HP compressor. Fan diameter 3.00 m (118 in). Compressor stages: single fan, 8 IP, 6 HP. Turbine stages: 1 HP, 2 IP, 6 LP. These facts apply to the XWB-84 in the listed family.
- [EASA E.111](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/downloads/7635/en) corroborated the architecture in search-index text, but direct retrieval returned HTTP 429. The application therefore cites the successfully retrieved CAA document instead.

## GE Aerospace GEnx-1B70 / Boeing 787-8

- [GE Aerospace, GEnx model comparison](https://www.geaerospace.com/commercial/aircraft-engines/genx), -1B70 (787-8) column: aircraft application, 111.1 in fan diameter; 1 fan / 4 booster / 10 HP compressor stages; 2 HP / 7 LP turbine stages. The site identifies the model as -1B70; the application does not infer a particular PIP or certification suffix.
- [NTSB, ENG24LA014, Powerplants Group Chair factual report, 25 July 2025](https://data.ntsb.gov/Docket/Document/docBLOB?FileExtension=pdf&FileName=ENG24LA014+-+Powerplant+Factual+Report+FINAL+July2025-Rel.pdf&ID=19028934), page 17, section 1.3: generic GEnx-1B description confirms two rotors, HP turbine driving HP compressor, LP turbine driving fan and LP compressor (booster). The report's accident-specific engine is a different variant; no incident-specific rating, component condition, blade count or installation claim is transferred to the -1B70.
- [EASA IM.E.102](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/downloads/7641/en) was found but direct retrieval returned HTTP 429. It is not used as the principal connection source.

- [NASA Glenn, Turbofan Engine](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/Animation/turbtyp/etft.html): core and bypass streams, fan turbine and shaft, bypass ratio and thrust.

## Deliberate boundaries

No original engineering geometry is claimed. One rotating symbol represents each component assembly, not a blade row or stage count. The flow split, distances and areas are illustrative. Fan diameter and stage counts appear as specifications in comparison only; they do not set the drawing's scale.

No thrust ratings, bypass ratios, pressure ratios, real RPM, temperature limits or fuel rates are used to calibrate this application. Gauges are dimensionless 0–100 teaching indices, generated by one shared response model. They cannot compare engine efficiency or power. A zero temperature index denotes the model's resting state, not zero degrees. Startup, shutdown, thermal lag and relative animation rates are invented teaching behaviour and are labelled accordingly, not engineering facts.

Temperature display update (21 November 2026): numerical Celsius/Fahrenheit examples are now available at the user's request. They use a deliberately chosen 20 °C ambient plus 12 °C per relative heat-index point, not researched engine readings. Fahrenheit is the exact 9/5 Celsius + 32 unit conversion before display rounding. Earlier statements about relative-only display describe the initial version. No operating limits or genuine compartment temperatures are claimed.
